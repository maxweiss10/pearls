Mechanism — central μ-receptor effect, not histamine (exception: morphine releases histamine → urticaria/flushing). Benadryl is ineffective unless there is a rash or true allergic reaction.
First look — confirm itch without rash (rash/hives/wheeze → treat as drug reaction, not opioid pruritus) · check pain control, sedation, RR · note which opioid and route (neuraxial and morphine are the usual culprits)
Overnight sequence
Step / doseNotes
1. Nalbuphine (Nubain) — mixed κ-agonist / μ-antagonist
5 mg IV q6h prn (2.5 mg if elderly or frail)
White Book first-line · reverses itch, preserves analgesia · repeat once at 30–60 min if needed
Can precipitate withdrawal in high-dose or chronic opioid users
2. Ondansetron (Zofran) — 5-HT3RA
4 mg IV
Safe add-on, usually already on the MAR · best evidence with neuraxial opioids, modest for systemic
Check QTc
3. Rotate or reduce — e.g. morphine → hydromorphone or fentanyl
↓ 25–50% on rotation
Side effects with pain relief → rotate with dose reduction · side effects without pain relief → rotate IV opioid, no reduction · morphine is the histamine offender, prefer hydromorphone/fentanyl
4. Naloxone gtt — low dose, sub-reversal
0.25 mcg/kg/hr (up to 1 mcg/kg/hr)
Severe or refractory · compatible for simultaneous administration with morphine · needs pump and order set — best for PCA patients
Caution with high-dose ER opioids; watch for pain recurrence
5. Antihistamine — sedative benefit only
Hydroxyzine 25 mg PO or diphenhydramine 12.5–25 mg PO
Last resort if the goal is sleep · avoid IV Benadryl in favor of PO · sedation stacks with the opioid
Skip overnight
Propofol (subhypnotic 10–20 mg, brief effect) · Naltrexone PO (neuraxial prophylaxis only, shortens analgesia) · Mirtazapine / gabapentin (chronic or palliative settings)
Chronic / sickle cell
Non-sedating antihistamine or 5-HT3RA outpatient · inpatient naloxone gtt 0.25 mcg/kg/hr alongside morphine or PCA
Handoff
Flag for day team — scheduled nalbuphine prn, opioid rotation, or pall care if refractory

Calcium Albumin Correction (JAMA Netw Open 2025;8(1):e2455251)
  • Bottom line: calcium albumin correction is not real. Adjusting made agreement with ionized calcium worse, not better
  • Design: population-based cross-sectional study, Alberta. 22,658 adults with total calcium and ionized calcium drawn at the same time, 2013–2019, across inpatient, ED and outpatient settings
  • Agreement with ionized calcium (the reference standard):
    • Unadjusted total Ca 74.5%
    • Simplified Payne 63.0%
    • Original Payne 58.7%
  • Why it matters: misclassification was highest exactly where the formula gets reached for — albumin <30 g/L, i.e. the hypoalbuminemic patient who is the whole reason anyone corrects
  • So: use the unadjusted total calcium as the practical alternative, or measure an ionized calcium when it actually matters
Desgagnés N, King JA, Kline GA, Seiden-Long I, Leung AA. Use of Albumin-Adjusted Calcium Measurements in Clinical Practice. JAMA Netw Open. 2025;8(1):e2455251. Erratum corrects only an eGFR unit label; the agreement figures are unchanged.

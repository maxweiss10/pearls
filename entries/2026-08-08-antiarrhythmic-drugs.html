Class I: sodium channel blockers
  1. The top trace is a normal ventricular action potential, phases 0–4, with the effective refractory period marked. Each Class I subclass bends it differently: 1a prolongs the action potential duration, 1b shortens it, 1c barely changes it.

Agents and what they cost you: procainamide (hypotension, lupus-like syndrome), quinidine (GI, cinchonism, thrombocytopenia), disopyramide (anticholinergic, avoid in heart failure); lidocaine (second line in MI arrhythmias, neuro and cardiovascular effects), phenytoin; flecainide and propafenone (dizziness, visual disturbance, exacerbate heart failure, prolong QT).
  4. Two things to carry off the page: Class 1a can induce torsades, and Class 1c is proarrhythmic — not with structural heart disease.
Classes II–V
  1. Class II, beta blockers: slow SA and AV nodal conduction, inhibit phase 4 depolarization, prolong PR. Propranolol, metoprolol, nadolol, timolol, esmolol. Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm.
  2. Class III, potassium channel blockers: delay repolarization, prolonging APD, ERP and QT. Amiodarone carries the long tail (liver toxicity, interstitial pneumonitis, corneal microdeposits, photosensitivity, thyroid disorders); dronedarone; sotalol (torsades).
  3. Class IV, calcium channel blockers: verapamil and diltiazem, prolong PR. Avoid in HFrEF, caution with beta blockers.
  4. Others: adenosine for SVT, magnesium for torsades and digoxin toxicity, potassium, and digoxin for atrial fibrillation and flutter via Na/K ATPase inhibition.

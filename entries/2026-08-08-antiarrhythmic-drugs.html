Class I — Sodium Channel Blockers
Reading the figure
  • Top trace: a normal ventricular action potential, phases 0–4, with the effective refractory period marked. Each subclass bends it differently — 1a prolongs the action potential duration, 1b shortens it, 1c barely changes it
  • The table compares 1a, 1b and 1c across uses, Na⁺ and K⁺ channel effects, APD, ERP, QRS and QT
  • Agents and what they cost you: procainamide (hypotension, lupus-like syndrome), quinidine (GI, cinchonism, thrombocytopenia), disopyramide (anticholinergic, avoid in heart failure); lidocaine (2nd line in MI arrhythmias, neuro and CV effects), phenytoin; flecainide and propafenone (dizziness, visual disturbance, exacerbate heart failure, prolong QT)
  • Carry off the page: Class 1a can induce torsades. Class 1c is proarrhythmic — not with structural heart disease
Classes II–V
Reading the figure
  • Class II, beta blockers: slow SA and AV nodal conduction, inhibit phase 4 depolarization, prolong PR. Propranolol, metoprolol, nadolol, timolol, esmolol. Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm
  • Class III, potassium channel blockers: delay repolarization, prolonging APD, ERP and QT. Amiodarone carries the long tail (liver toxicity, interstitial pneumonitis, corneal microdeposits, photosensitivity, thyroid disorders); dronedarone; sotalol (torsades)
  • Class IV, calcium channel blockers: verapamil and diltiazem, prolong PR. Avoid in HFrEF, caution with beta blockers
  • Others: adenosine for SVT; magnesium for torsades and digoxin toxicity; potassium; digoxin for atrial fibrillation and flutter via Na/K ATPase inhibition

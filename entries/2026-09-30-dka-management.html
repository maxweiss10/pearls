DKA Algorithm, Simplified (full UpToDate algorithm)

The four treatment arms run at the same time. Every branch, footnote and lab list lives in the full algorithm linked above.

DKA

1 L NS or LR over 1 h
Blood gas, BMP, CBC, UA, ECG; find the trigger; corrected Na: add 2 per 100 mg/dL glucose over 100

NS or LR ~1 L/h
Rate by exam; shock → pressors

Normal or high → ½NS; low → NS; 250–500 mL/h

Potassium
Confirm UOP ≥50 mL/h
<3.5: HOLD insulin; KCl 10–20 mEq/h until >3.5
3.5–5: KCl 10–20 mEq per L of fluid, goal 4–5
>5: none; recheck q2h
Insulin, once K >3.5
Regular 0.1 U/kg/h IV
Bolus 0.1 U/kg only if the drip is delayed
Uncomplicated: rapid-acting SUBQ 0.1 U/kg, then 0.1 U/kg q1h or 0.2 U/kg q2h
NOT DOWN 50–70 IN HOUR 1↓
Double the rate
Fall must not exceed 90–120 mg/dL/h

Bicarbonate
Only if pH <7.0
NaHCO₃ 100 mEq in 400 mL over 2 h; add 20 mEq KCl if K <5; repeat q2h until pH >7.0

SUBQ basal-bolus
Keep the drip 1–2 h after the first rapid-acting SUBQ dose (2–4 h if short- or long-acting); insulin-naïve TDD 0.5–0.6 U/kg/day. Until then: lytes, BUN, pH, phos, Cr, glucose q2–4h; BOHB q2h
Reading the full algorithm (open image)
  • Top box: initial evaluation, then the four columns branch off it and run together
  • Column 1, IV fluids: volume status (severe / mild / cardiogenic shock) → correct the deficit within 24 h → fluid by corrected Na → dextrose once glucose <250
  • Column 2, potassium: kidney function first, then three K bands (<3.5 / 3.5–5 / >5)
  • Column 3, insulin: IV for moderate–severe, SUBQ for uncomplicated; both feed the same titration box and the glucose <250 step
  • Column 4, bicarbonate: pH <7.0 vs ≥7.0
  • Bottom boxes: fluids and insulin columns both end in the identical monitoring and SUBQ-transition box
  • Footnote marks: * initial labs and studies; ¶ corrected-Na formula; Δ a variable-rate nurse-driven drip may replace the fixed 0.1 U/kg/h
